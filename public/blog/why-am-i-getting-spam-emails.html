Why Am I Suddenly Getting Spam Emails? Causes + Fixes

Quiet inbox on Monday, forty junk emails by Friday — and you changed nothing. Infuriating, but rarely mysterious. Your address leaked through one of five usual doors, and each one has a specific fix. Let's find yours.

Cause 1 — A breach you never heard about

The most common one by far. Some service you used years ago got breached, and your address is now circulating in combo lists. Check yourself on a breach-lookup service; if you appear (most active emails do), the spam isn't your fault — but the password reuse it enables would be. Change that password everywhere it appears, starting with your email itself.

Cause 2 — You fed a list farmer

That coupon popup, that "free" ebook, that contest entry. Lead-harvesting pages exist to collect addresses and sell them onward — legally gray, practically unstoppable once sold. Think back to what you signed up for right before the flood started. That's usually your answer, and the reason burner addresses exist for exactly these moments.

Cause 3 — Your address was guessed

Short, common addresses get dictionary spam — firstname.lastname at big providers receives junk meant for nobody in particular. There's no fix for other people's guesses except brutal filtering, which is one more argument for keeping your real address boring and private while burners take the public hits.

Cause 4 — You confirmed you're alive

Clicked "unsubscribe" on obvious spam? Replied "stop"? Opened with images loading? Each tells the sender the address is live and monitored, and live addresses sell at a premium. Real companies get real unsubscribes; obvious junk gets marked as spam, blocked, never touched. The full drill is in the unsubscribe guide.

Cause 5 — Someone scraped you

Posted your address publicly — forum signature, GitHub profile, marketplace listing, conference badge PDF? Scrapers harvest those constantly. Get it off public pages, and use a burner for anything that must stay visible.

The cleanup that sticks

Short term: filter hard (auto-archive anything with "unsubscribe" that you didn't ask for), report pure spam instead of engaging, and fix any breached passwords. Long term: stop the inflow at the source — new signups go to disposable addresses, full stop. An inbox can recover from any of these causes. It just can't recover while you're still feeding it new senders every week.

Quick answers

Why did spam spike suddenly? Usually a breach dump going live or one bad signup selling your address onward. Check breach lookups first.

Will changing my email help? Only with new habits attached — a fresh address fed to the same list farmers ends up the same way.

Should I reply STOP to spam texts? No — same trap as spam unsubscribes. Block and report instead.
