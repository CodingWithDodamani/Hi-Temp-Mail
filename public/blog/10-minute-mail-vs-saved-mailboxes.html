10-Minute Mail vs Saved Mailboxes: Which Throwaway Wins in 2026

Classic 10-minute mail gives you an inbox with a death sentence: ten minutes, then everything vanishes. Saved mailboxes flip the model — the same address comes back days later with new mail still arriving. Both are "temporary email," but they fail in opposite ways. Here's how to pick the right one for the job.

How classic 10-minute mail works

You land on the page, an address exists, a countdown runs. When it hits zero (or you close the tab), the mailbox and its messages are destroyed. It's perfect for single-shot verifications: grab one OTP, confirm, walk away. The weakness shows up the moment anything repeats — a second code tomorrow, a re-login check, a "verify it's really you" email next week. The address is gone, and with it any account anchored to it.

How saved mailboxes change the deal

Hi Temp Mail starts with the same 10-minute lifetime and Extend button — then adds a vault. Tap the bookmark under your address and the mailbox credentials are stored in your browser only. Days later, open Saved, tap the address, and the exact same inbox is back: old messages where the provider kept them, and crucially, new mail and OTPs still arriving. Session restore keeps your current mailbox across reloads too. Nothing about this is an account on our side — there is no server, no login, nothing to breach.

Head-to-head

Lifetime: 10-minute mail wins on guaranteed forgetting; saved mailboxes win on every real-world timeline longer than an hour.
OTP reliability: equal for the first code; saved mailboxes win every repeat code by a mile.
Lockout risk: 10-minute mail practically guarantees lockout if a service re-verifies; saved mailboxes reduce it to provider-side expiry only.
Privacy: equal — both keep your real address out of the signup; expiry speed is the only difference.
Effort: one extra tap to bookmark. That's the entire price of the vault.

The honest limits of saved mailboxes

Saved doesn't mean permanent. Mailboxes live on the provider's servers and can be retired after long inactivity; a cleared browser cache wipes your vault unless you exported a backup from Settings. Treat saved addresses as weeks-to-months tools, and graduate anything truly important to a permanent inbox. For the full risk picture, read Is Temp Mail Safe?

Verdict

One code, one minute, never again: classic 10-minute style is fine. Anything else — trials, testing, social signups, repeat logins — save the mailbox. Since bookmarking costs one tap, there's rarely a reason not to. Try it live: generate an address, bookmark it, come back tomorrow.

Quick answers

How long do saved mailboxes last? Days to weeks in practice; restore anytime from Saved while the provider keeps the address.

Do OTPs arrive in restored mailboxes? Yes — new mail lands normally after restore.

What if I clear browser data? The vault is wiped. Export a backup from Settings → Backup first.
